What Markdown is

You type ## A lemma and get a heading. You type *convex* and get convex. That is nearly all of it — the rules fit on the back of a postcard, and you can see them all further down this page.

The point is what the file stays: plain text. It is readable exactly as you typed it, even with nothing to render it. It opens in any editor on any machine, it goes into version control sensibly, and it will still open in twenty years. Formatting, without the words being locked inside a program.

Formulas are TeX. Write $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$ inside a sentence, or put $$…$$ on its own lines for a displayed equation. If you already write TeX, you already know the part that matters — the Markdown around it is the small tax.

Why this site exists

Two ordinary situations, both awkward without a tool at hand.

Someone sent you a .md file. Opened in a text editor it looks like source code — stray asterisks, hash marks, dollar signs. You want to read it, not decode it.

You have heard Markdown is convenient and would like to find out in a minute rather than an afternoon — without installing an editor, creating an account or reading a manual first.

So everything here runs in your browser. Your notes stay on your own machine and are uploaded nowhere unless you explicitly ask for it.

When you outgrow this

This site is a doorway, not a workshop. Once Markdown starts earning its keep, these are where people go — and every one of them reads and writes the same .md files, so nothing you write here is trapped.

  • VS Code — free and open source, and the usual choice if you also write code. Previews Markdown out of the box; an extension adds TeX.
  • Obsidian — free for personal use. Turns a folder of .md files into a linked notebook; TeX included. Widely used by researchers.
  • Zettlr — free and open source, built for academic writing: citations, footnotes, export to LaTeX and Word.
  • Joplin — free and open source. A notebook that syncs between your own devices, with formulas and no account required.
  • HackMD — in the browser, free to use, and collaborative — the one to reach for when writing with a co-author. Does formulas and slides.

Notes live in this browser, on this computer. Nothing leaves it unless you ask for it.
