Help

Most questions about lemma-md come from one fact: it runs entirely in your browser. Here is what that means.

Is there a server? Do I need an account?

No, and no. lemma-md is a set of files — pages, scripts, fonts — that your browser downloads, as it does for any website. From then on everything happens in your browser: the editor, the typesetting of formulas, the slides. There is no lemma-md server for your notes to go to, so they go to none.

That is also why there is no sign-up. An account would only be needed to keep your notes somewhere of ours, and there is no such place. A note leaves your computer only when you save it to Google Drive, and then it goes straight from your browser to Google.

GitHub, which serves the files, sees that your browser asked for them, as any web host does; it never sees your notes. The details are in the privacy policy. The other side of having no server: nobody keeps a copy for you. See where your notes are before you save them.

Can I open and save files on my computer?

Yes, in Chrome, Edge, Opera and other browsers built on Chromium, on a computer. There lemma-md works like a desktop editor: Open a file… opens a .md file, you edit it, and saving writes back into that same file. After a reload the browser may ask once more whether lemma-md may edit it.

This rests on a browser feature called the File System Access API. Firefox and Safari have chosen not to add it, judging that letting a web page write into files on your disk is too easy to abuse. Some Chromium browsers, such as Brave, switch it off as well, and browsers on phones and tablets do not have it.

In Firefox, Safari, and on a phone

  • Opening works: drag a file onto the page, or use Open a file….
  • Saving downloads a copy (Download as .md), into your Downloads folder unless the browser asks where to put it.
  • lemma-md cannot write over a file on your disk. To carry on with a downloaded copy later, open it again.

In every browser, notes are also kept in the browser as you type, and Google Drive works the same everywhere.

Where are my notes before I save them?

In your browser’s storage for this site, called IndexedDB. Every site has its own, and no other site can read it. lemma-md writes each note there as you type, a moment after you stop.

Think of the recovery files a desktop editor keeps. Close the tab, quit the browser, restart the computer: your notes are back in their tabs when you return. You do not have to save a note to go on with it tomorrow.

But this is not a copy to rely on:

  • It belongs to this browser on this device. Another browser, another computer or your phone sees none of it.
  • Clearing the browser’s data for this site (“cookies and site data”) deletes it.
  • A private or incognito window keeps it only until the window closes.
  • When the disk runs low, the browser may delete a site’s data to make room, unless the site was allowed to keep it. Settings → Keep them permanently asks for that.
  • Safari deletes a site’s data once you have not visited it for seven days of using Safari.

So for anything that matters, save the note to a file or to Google Drive.

The browser’s storage holds the notes open in your tabs, and the bin. A saved note leaves it when you close its tab, since its file is now the copy. A note you close with Discard changes goes to the bin, in Settings, for 15 days, in case you change your mind.

lemma-md says it can’t store anything. How do I allow it?

No browser blocks this by default. If lemma-md says it can’t store anything, the browser was set to stop sites from keeping data on your device: in its settings, by an extension, or by whoever manages the computer. Usually it comes with blocking all cookies, because browsers treat a site’s storage like its cookies.

Until it is allowed, lemma-md still works but keeps nothing, so save or download each note before you close the tab. Allowing lemma-md alone is enough; the rest of the web can stay blocked. When you have changed the setting, reload the lemma-md page — after saving your notes, since while storage is blocked a reload loses them.

The site to allow is lemma-md.github.io.

Chrome

  1. Open Settings → Privacy and security → Site settings → Additional content settings → On-device site data, or type chrome://settings/content/siteData into the address bar.
  2. For lemma-md alone: next to Allowed to save data on your device, click Add and enter the site.
  3. Or, for every site: under Default behavior, choose Allow sites to save data on your device.

If your notes vanish each time you quit Chrome, the same page has Delete data sites have saved to your device when you close all windows selected. Adding lemma-md to the allowed sites fixes that too.

Edge

  1. Open Settings → Cookies and site permissions → Manage and delete cookies and site data, or type edge://settings/content/cookies into the address bar.
  2. For lemma-md alone: next to Allow, click Add and enter the site.
  3. Or, for every site: turn on Allow sites to save and read cookie data.

Firefox

  1. Open Settings → Privacy & Security, and under Cookies and Site Data click Manage Exceptions….
  2. Enter the site, click Allow, then Save Changes.

An exception also keeps your notes when Delete cookies and site data when Firefox is closed is on.

Safari

  1. On a Mac: Safari → Settings → Privacy, and turn off Block all cookies.
  2. On an iPhone or iPad: in the Settings app, open Safari (under Apps on recent versions) and turn off Block All Cookies.

Safari has no exception for a single site.

If that is not it

  • A setting that is greyed out, or marked as managed by your organisation, can be changed only by whoever manages the computer.
  • A privacy or cookie-blocking extension can do the same. Allow lemma-md in it, or try a window where extensions are off.
